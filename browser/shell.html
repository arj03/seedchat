    Rooms

      You can be in several rooms at once, and are linked to everyone in each. Add peer beside someone in a room adds them to your peers below: you stay linked whether or not you share a room. A room has no secret: whoever has its name, or its link, can join. Random gives the name typed an ending nobody can guess, for a room that should stay private.

      ⇄

      Peers

      • Nobody yet: add a peer from a room, or by its link or key.

      The peers you are connected to directly, by key, whether or not you share a room. direct is a link that has moved to WebRTC; via relay is one the relay still forwards.

      A contact is reached directly, by its key, with no room needed. One with a contact secret answers only a caller that presents it, so a contact with no answer may need its secret entered on its row.

      My nick

      What you call yourself. Your peers are told it, and their shell and every app show it in place of your key. It is your own word, not proof of who you are: anyone can take any nick, and the key is what identifies a peer.

      My contact secret

      Room names take A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ -. Joining with none typed takes you to the default global room. Links carry what they share in the # fragment, which browsers never send to a server, so the relay learns neither a room's name nor a contact secret from one.

      Start a relay locally with npm run relay, or point at a public one your group trusts.

      A relay secret is the relay's own, not the room's: a relay started with --secret serves only clients that prove it, and the proof never sends the secret. Get it from whoever runs the relay; it is not part of any link. Leave it empty for an open relay.
